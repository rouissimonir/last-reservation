## People, relationships and knowledge

- **Elin Ward / Ruth Vane**, visitor and reporter: knows her alias, genuine folio, Victor's contradiction and her own voluntary plan. Trusts the guests as independent witnesses. She has not accused anyone of kidnapping her.
- **Victor Hale**, owner and suspect: wants the sale to proceed under a false vacancy declaration. Knows the folio exchange and lies when telling reception Elin cancelled. Knows Elin reached the archive; does not know her chosen retreat. His authenticated words prove deliberate document substitution, not abduction.
- **Mara Vale**, duty manager and suspect: fears losing her job if the sale fails, but maintains accurate appointment/access records. Knows the alias at 21:10 and later sees the westward route. Does not know the folio substitution or room name before investigating. Her suspicion of a forced departure is a worry, not evidence.
- **Owen Bell**, porter and suspect: wants to protect a visitor from a confrontation. Knows the archive escort, later directions and tea delivery. Does not know the fraudulent buyer declaration until the supplied documents are compared. His assistance does not establish authorship of the false booking.
- **Celia Rook**, archivist and suspect: resents Victor's treatment of the archive and wants its records preserved. Knows the true reservation, Elin's identity and message authorship. The case's verified records, including her custody/authentication notes, are reliable; no hidden collusion is required to solve it.

## Reliability

Direct observations in witness statements are accurate within their stated limits. The badge transcript records exact words, but the caller's claim that a visitor cancelled is a deliberate lie. The current appointment page is known to have been altered; only the sealed carbon and authentication/access/facility records are certified. A character's guess or accusation is not automatically true. There are no unseen visitors, identity swaps, borrowed badges or unlisted doors relevant to the proof.

Question: Which appointment name did Elin use? A1: personal introduction and simultaneous green brooch/folio-72 request; witness cannot read appointment name. ### LR4 · Act II: The altered ledger

Prerequisite LR3; references LR-A4, LR-B4.

Hints: distinguish the escort from the author; identify the caller using the personal call log and compare the carbon; V17 is Victor and he admits the false swap.

### LR5 · Act III: A visitor found

B5: complete floor-plan destinations: west/ground = parcel room; east/first = music room; west/first = staff reading room. Answer **West staff reading room**. Branch alone or floor alone each leaves two possibilities. The question asks the chosen room, not a guarantee that she can never leave it. Prerequisite LR4; references LR-A5, LR-B5.

Hints: use both branch and floor; ground-west and first-east each fail one condition; west plus first is the staff reading room.

### LR6 · Act III: A visitor found

Question: What do W6 and its custody record support? A6: text W6, claimed signature E.W., says she chose to retreat, can leave, holds originals and awaits independent witnesses. Without B, author is unverified. B6: Celia witnessed Elin write/sign W6 at 21:59 after press-card identity verification; message custody intact. The words alone leave genuine or forged; provenance alone leaves the two voluntary plans. Together they select one explanation. Prerequisite LR5; references LR-A6, LR-B6.

Hints: distinguish message content from provenance; compare the actual plan with the witnessed authorship record; authenticated Elin explains a voluntary retreat to protect originals and await independent witnesses.

## Walkthrough and ending

## Adversarial logic pass before integration

- Do not identify by brooch alone; retain a second same-brooch candidate and a second same-folio candidate.
- Alias could refer to another person: explicitly connect A1's introduced identity, B1's unique appointment row and A2's continuously observed named visitor. No unseen identity exchange.
- “Single bell” could describe a double bell partially heard: A2 observes the whole opening and expressly hears no second note.
- Guide could hand over halfway: A3 explicitly describes the same continuous escort and no change of guide/lanyard.
- Presence alone could falsely imply culpability: LR3 establishes escort only; LR4 uses attributed direct admission plus the original record to establish alteration.
- Badges could be borrowed: B4 records a clerk observing Victor personally place this call; not just badge possession.
- Door freedom alone could falsely imply willing retreat: LR6 needs authenticated first-person words, not just the door inspection.
- A signed message could be forged: authorship is independently witnessed and verified in B6; neither unauthenticated signature nor hearsay suffices.
- Ending-only motive clue: all essential willingness/reason is in LR-A6/B6 before the final answer. Reveal adds closure, not a missing premise.
- Alternative rooms: B5 declares its route/floor table exhaustive for these service branches; none matches both constraints except reading room.
